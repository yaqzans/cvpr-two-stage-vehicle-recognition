## 1. The research question

> Does a two-step detect-then-classify pipeline recognise Bangladesh's look-alike local vehicles (rickshaw vs rickshaw_van, truck vs covered_van, etc.) better than a single all-in-one detector, and what does that decoupling cost at inference time?

**Architecture:**
```
road scene (1920x1080) → Stage 1: YOLO detector (where?) → crop each box →
Stage 2: ConvNeXt-Tiny classifier (what?) → final label
```

Critically: **Stage 1's class prediction is discarded.** Only the bounding box survives. Stage 2 re-labels every crop from scratch. This is the load-bearing design decision behind every metric-definition fight documented below (see §4.1).

## 2. Dataset

### 2.1 RSUD20K (detection dataset, Stage 1 input)

- **Source:** H. Zunair, S. Khan, A. Ben Hamza, "RSUD20K: a dataset for road scene understanding in autonomous driving," IEEE ICIP, 2024.
- **Local path:** `Datasets/rsud20k/` — `images/{train,val,test}/`, `labels/{train,val,test}/` (YOLO-format `.txt`), `classes.txt`
- **Also mirrored on Kaggle** (public): `hasibzunair/rsud20k-bangladesh-road-scene-understanding` — same structure, nested one level deeper: `<dataset>/rsud20k/images/train/...`
- **Resolution:** 1920×1080
- **13 classes** (in class-id order): `person, rickshaw, rickshaw_van, auto_rickshaw, truck, pickup_truck, private_car, motorcycle, bicycle, bus, micro_bus, covered_van, human_hauler`

**Split sizes (verified, matches local copy and Kaggle mirror exactly):**

| Split | Images | Boxes | Boxes/img |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 18,681 | 118,810 | 6.4 |
| val | 1,004 | 7,385 | 7.4 |
| test | 649 | 3,805 | 5.9 |

**Class imbalance (train split): 72:1.** `person` = 32,884 boxes, `human_hauler` = 455 boxes. Nine of 13 classes have under 250 test objects. This single fact drives: Stage 2's class-weighted loss, Stage 1's rare-class oversampling (26s recipe onward), and why macro-averaged metrics on this benchmark are noisy (see §4.5, mAP retraction).

**Object scale:** person and motorcycle are the smallest objects on average (median box area, see `fig02_object_size_distribution`), which is why the detector struggles most on them at 640px input (3x downscale from native 1920×1080).

**Verification script:** `scripts/01_verify_rsud20k.py` — checks image/label pairing, class-id ranges, box-coordinate sanity.

Test split of the classification dataset = 3,805 ground-truth crops (one per RSUD20K test-split box), used throughout as the "clean" evaluation set that isolates Stage 2's own accuracy from Stage 1's detection errors.

## 4. Stage 2 — classifier: every variant tried

### 4.1 Why crop recall is measured the way it is (read this before touching Stage 1 metrics)

The two-stage design **discards Stage 1's class prediction**. Stage 2 re-labels every crop from scratch. This means the correct question for Stage 1 is "did you hand Stage 2 a usable box?" (class-agnostic IoU match), not "did you also guess the right class?" (class-aware match, what a single-stage detector is judged on). Every crop-recall table in this document reports both, but **agnostic is the pipeline-correct number** and is what should be quoted in the paper/poster.

The original crop-recall check, `scripts/scripts04_evaluate_stage1_crops.py`, is **superseded** — it required class-aware matching at conf=0.25 and is kept only because an early results write-up cites its 84.63% figure. The replacement, `scripts/05_eval_stage1_crop_recall.py`, is the pipeline-correct version used for every number in §3.

- **Script:** `scripts/train_stage2_convnext_tiny_fixed.py`
- **Model:** ConvNeXt-Tiny (`hf_hub:timm/convnext_tiny.in12k_ft_in1k`)
- **Preprocessing:** `Resize(256)` → `CenterCrop(224)` (this turned out to be the critical mistake — see §4.4)
- **Run dir:** `runs/stage2_convnext_tiny/` — **preserved, do not overwrite**, kept specifically for the v1-vs-v2 ablation

**Test metrics (on 3,805 ground-truth crops):** Accuracy 0.9466, Macro F1 0.8691, Macro precision 0.8892, Macro recall 0.8557. Best epoch 8/28, best val macro F1 0.7912.

### 4.3 Stage 2 v2 — current best

- **Script:** `scripts/train_stage2_convnext_tiny_v2.py` (~3.3h, 30 epochs)
- **Run dir:** `runs/stage2_convnext_tiny_v2/` — **current best, this is what production/the pipeline uses**
- **Three changes vs v1, all made together:**
  1. **`PadToSquare` + `Resize(224)`** replacing `Resize(256)`+`CenterCrop(224)`. The v1 crop discarded most of every non-square box: measured survival fraction of the long dimension — person 32%, rickshaw 57%, bicycle 58%, motorcycle 61%, bus 68%, truck 72%. A person crop became torso-only.
  2. **Cosine schedule `T_max=30`** matched to the real training budget (v1 used `T_max=100` but stopped at epoch 28, so LR only fell from 1.00e-4 to 8.3e-5 — never reached the low-LR regime). Early stopping disabled, 2-epoch warmup.
  3. **`RandomBoxJitter(max_frac=0.12, p=0.8)`** — v1 trained only on exact human-drawn boxes but is deployed on YOLO boxes at ~0.89 mean IoU; this augmentation exposes it to jittered boxes during training.

**Test metrics:** Accuracy **0.9703** (+0.024 vs v1), Macro F1 **0.8940** (+0.025), Macro precision 0.9085, Macro recall 0.8848. Best epoch 17/30, best val macro F1 0.8552 (+0.064 vs v1).

**Confirmed on real detector crops (not just ground-truth crops)** — this is the number that actually matters, since in deployment Stage 2 never sees a perfect ground-truth crop:

| Single-stage baseline | 0.7432 | 0.9148 |
| Two-stage + v1 classifier | 0.7443 | 0.9346 |
| **Two-stage + v2 classifier** | **0.7595** | **0.9590** |

With v1, the two-stage system was barely better than single-stage on mAP (0.7443 vs 0.7432) — **the preprocessing fix (item 1 above) is what makes the two-stage design worth its cost.**

### 4.4 Preprocessing ablation detail (why v1→v2 worked)

This is worth restating precisely since it's the single highest-leverage finding in the whole project: **the bug was not the model, it was the crop resize.** `Resize(256)+CenterCrop(224)` assumes roughly-square input; RSUD20K boxes are frequently tall-and-thin (person) or wide-and-flat (bus/truck side profile), so center-cropping to a square discards most of the informative pixels along the long axis. `PadToSquare` (pad the short axis with grey, then resize) keeps 100% of every box's content, just at lower relative resolution along the previously-short axis. See `fig11_preprocessing_comparison` for the visual side-by-side.

### 4.5 Robustness check — does the improvement replicate on validation? (the mAP retraction)

Everything in §4.3 was re-run on the untouched **validation** split (1,004 images, 7,385 objects) to check what survives.

**What replicates (robust, report this):**

| Metric | Test: single→two-stage | Val: single→two-stage |
|---|---|---|
| **End-to-end recognition accuracy** | 0.9148→**0.9590** (+0.044) | 0.8600→**0.9039** (+0.044) |

Identical to three decimals across splits. **12 of 13 classes move in the same direction on both splits**, including the headline cases: covered_van +0.333 test / +0.350 val, human_hauler +0.333/+0.348, bicycle +0.215/+0.178, pickup_truck +0.400/+0.068, micro_bus +0.219/+0.046.

**What does NOT replicate (retracted claim):**

| Metric | Test: single→two-stage | Val: single→two-stage |
|---|---|---|
| mAP50 | 0.7432→0.7595 (**+0.016**) | 0.6909→0.6739 (**−0.017**) |
| mAP50-95 | 0.5974→0.6131 (+0.016) | 0.5497→0.5325 (−0.017) |

**The mAP advantage reverses sign on validation.** Root cause: mAP depends on how well the *confidence score ranks* detections across the whole PR curve; the two-stage score is `det_conf × cls_prob`, and the classifier probability (trained with label smoothing + class weights) distorts that ranking. Per-class evidence in `runs/pipeline_eval/METRIC_STABILITY.csv` (reproduced in full in §4.5.1 below) shows 8 small classes flipping sign of `delta_ap50` between val and test — this is sampling noise on classes with 23-241 test objects, not a real effect. **End-to-end recognition accuracy is identical across all three score-fusion choices tested** (det_conf×cls_prob / det_conf-only / cls_prob-only) — the argmax class never changes, only the AP ranking does.

Alternative score fusions tested, none fixed the mAP instability:

| Final score | Test mAP50 | Val mAP50 |
|---|---|---|
| Single-stage (detector confidence) | 0.7432 | 0.6909 |
| `det_conf × cls_prob` (default, two-stage) | 0.7595 | 0.6739 |
| `det_conf` only | 0.7523 | 0.6575 |
| `cls_prob` only | — | 0.1689 |

**Honest framing used in the poster/paper:** lead with recognition accuracy (robust), report mAP on both splits, explicitly state the calibration/ranking limitation as a retracted secondary claim rather than silently dropping it.

### 4.6 Open problem: truck is unsolved

- 29 test / 65 val objects, 1,506 train objects.
- Stage 1 localizes only 75.86% of trucks and this does not improve at any confidence threshold (~7/29 never detected).
- Stage 2 reaches its best-in-project F1 (0.5417) on truck, and it's still the worst class.
- **Two-stage end-to-end is genuinely worse than single-stage for this one class, on both splits** — not sampling noise, a real effect. The classifier confuses truck with covered_van more than the detector's own head does.
- Root cause judged to be a **data problem**: genuinely ambiguous Bangladeshi truck/covered-van body types at very low sample counts, not a preprocessing or model-capacity problem.

### 4.7 Open problem: RSUD20K's annotations are incomplete

At conf=0.10, the detector produces ~2.7 unmatched (false-positive) boxes per frame. Inspecting 1,762 of them: they are overwhelmingly **real, unlabelled vehicles/people**, not hallucinations. Objective check: running Stage 2 over these unmatched crops, it agreed with the detector's own class guess **72.9%** of the time, vs **7.7%** expected by chance if they were noise.

**Consequences:**
- Measured precision is a floor — the system is being penalized for objectively correct detections RSUD20K never labelled.
- No model can reach 100% on this benchmark as constructed.
- **A background/reject class must never be trained on these "false positive" crops** — doing so would teach the classifier to actively ignore real rickshaws etc. If a reject class is wanted, it needs to be built from independently-verified empty regions.

- **Rationale:** v2's `RandomBoxJitter` could only pad with grey when expanding a box, because v2's crops are cut exactly at the ground-truth boundary with zero surrounding context. The margin dataset gives jitter real pixels to land on.

## 6. Kaggle cloud-training infrastructure (built this session)

This entire subsystem was built to train YOLO11m on Kaggle's free T4 GPU quota (30h/week) because the local 4060 Ti was needed for YOLO12m simultaneously, and because the user wanted a second, independently-trained medium-backbone comparison. **This section documents the infrastructure and every bug fixed while building it — this is reusable for any future Kaggle training job on this project.**

### 6.1 Files

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `scripts/kaggle/build_notebook.py` | Generates the actual training notebook (`.ipynb`) from Python source strings — never hand-edit notebook JSON (escaping bugs). Produces 4 cells: environment/GPU check, offline ultralytics install, the actual training logic (dataset discovery, oversampling, resume detection, `model.train()`), completion check (writes `DONE.flag` only if the true epoch target was reached). |
| `scripts/kaggle/push_and_run.py` | The local driver: validates the notebook (`ast.parse` every cell), writes `kernel-metadata.json`, pushes to Kaggle, polls status every 2 min, pulls output, decides whether to push a resume cycle. Has an `--attach` flag to reattach to an already-running kernel without re-pushing (critical for surviving local PC restarts without restarting the whole Kaggle job — see §6.4). |

### 6.2 Kaggle account setup required (one-time, user-side)

1. **Phone verification is mandatory** (`kaggle.com/settings`). Without it, kernels silently get **no GPU** (falls back to a CPU-only container, `torch...+cpu`) **and no internet access** — and the failure mode looks like a code bug, not an account restriction. This cost several debugging cycles before being identified (see §6.3, bugs 1-2).
2. **API token**: this project uses the new-style plain token (`KGAT_...`), stored at `~/.kaggle/access_token`, loaded into the environment per-command as `KAGGLE_API_TOKEN=$(cat ~/.kaggle/access_token)`. The new token format does **not** carry the username — `push_and_run.py`'s `get_username()` falls back to parsing `kaggle config view` output, or a legacy `kaggle.json` if present.
3. **Kaggle username for this project: `fairoozalammahi`**

### 6.3 Every bug hit while building this, in order

1. **Silent kernel death, zero-byte-feeling errors, no traceback.** Root cause: `enable_internet: true` in `kernel-metadata.json` combined with an unverified account → the pip install step inside the notebook fails with `gaierror: Temporary failure in name resolution` (DNS resolution itself doesn't work in the container). **Fix:** never rely on kernel internet access at all — set `enable_internet: false` and attach every dependency (ultralytics wheels, pretrained weights, the dataset) as Kaggle Datasets instead.
2. **GPU silently absent.** Even with `enable_gpu: true` and forcing `--accelerator NvidiaTeslaT4` on the push CLI command, the container came up with `torch 2.10.0+cpu, cuda available: False`. Root cause: same account-verification gate as bug 1 (no GPU without phone verification). **Fix:** user completed phone verification; confirmed working immediately after (`Tesla T4, cuda available: True`).
3. **`ultralytics` not preinstalled, and pip install has no internet (see bug 1) to fetch it.** **Fix:** built a fully offline install path — downloaded `ultralytics` (`--no-deps`, since Kaggle's base image already satisfies numpy/torch/opencv/etc.) plus its small missing deps (`polars`, `nvidia-ml-py`, `ultralytics-thop`, `ultralytics-platform`, `filelock`) locally, uploaded the ~2.5MB bundle as a private Kaggle dataset (`fairoozalammahi/ultralytics-offline-wheels`), and the notebook installs via `pip install --no-index --find-links <mounted-path> ultralytics`.
4. **Dataset mount path assumption wrong.** Expected `/kaggle/input/<slug>/...`; Kaggle actually nests it one level deeper: `/kaggle/input/datasets/<owner>/<slug>/...`. **Fix:** never hardcode the mount path — search for it by content (a known marker file/directory structure) instead.
5. **`Path.rglob("images/train")` (multi-segment recursive glob) unreliable** against the large (20k+ file), read-only, FUSE-mounted Kaggle dataset — returned nothing even though the identical target directory was found correctly via `os.walk` in a side-by-side diagnostic run seconds later. Switching to `glob.glob(..., recursive=True)` **also** failed the same way. **Fix: `os.walk` is the only method proven reliable in this specific environment** — used for all three dataset-discovery needs (RSUD20K root, offline wheels, checkpoint resume `last.pt`).
6. **Pretrained weights download fails** — `YOLO("yolo11m.pt")` tries to fetch from `github.com/ultralytics/assets/...` at model-construction time, which needs internet (disabled per bug 1's fix). **Fix:** downloaded `yolo11m.pt` and `yolo12m.pt` locally (~40MB each), uploaded as a private Kaggle dataset (`fairoozalammahi/yolo-pretrained-weights`), notebook locates the file by filename via `os.walk` and passes the **local path** to `YOLO(...)`, never the bare model name.
7. **Windows console crashes on `kaggle` CLI's own emoji output** (`'charmap' codec can't encode character '\u2705'` — a checkmark the Kaggle CLI itself prints). This silently truncated `kernels output` downloads (0-byte log files) whenever it happened mid-download. **Fix:** always invoke as `python -X utf8 -m kaggle ...`, never the bare `kaggle` executable, from any Windows shell.
8. **`kaggle datasets create` silently uploads nothing** ("Please upload at least one file") when the payload is entirely nested directories and no `-r`/`--dir-mode` flag is given (default appears to skip directories). **Fix:** always pass `-r zip` on `datasets create` and `datasets version` when uploading a directory tree (this was already correct for `version` but was a latent bug in the `create` first-time-push code path — fixed in `push_and_run.py`).
9. **CLI exits 0 even when a push is *rejected*** (e.g., "Maximum weekly GPU quota of 30.00 hours reached." / "Maximum batch GPU session count of 2 reached" are both real messages seen). **Never trust the exit code.** `push_and_run.py`'s `push()` function explicitly greps the literal string `"successfully pushed"` in the combined stdout+stderr and raises if it's absent — this caught the quota-exhaustion event correctly and prevented silently reporting a stale/old result as new.
10. **ultralytics' `EarlyStopping` does not survive `resume=True` across process restarts** — see §7.3, this bit both the Kaggle and local training paths, though on Kaggle it was moot since the notebook always restarts as a subprocess anyway with `time=` capping training length, not patience.
11. **P100 accelerator incompatibility (documented risk, avoided, not actually hit):** Kaggle's API-level `accelerator` field in `kernel-metadata.json` is silently ignored; only the CLI flag `--accelerator NvidiaTeslaT4` actually takes effect. The default (P100, CUDA capability 6.0) cannot run modern PyTorch builds (`(7.0)-(12.0)` required) — always pass `--accelerator NvidiaTeslaT4` explicitly on every push.

### 6.4 Surviving repeated PC restarts mid-training (this actually happened ~5 times)

Kaggle kernels run entirely on Kaggle's own servers once pushed — **turning the local PC off does not pause or stop an already-running Kaggle kernel.** What *does* die is the local Python process polling/monitoring it. Recovery procedure used every time (and should be used again):

```bash
export KAGGLE_API_TOKEN=$(cat ~/.kaggle/access_token)
python -X utf8 -m kaggle kernels status fairoozalammahi/stage1-yolo11m   # check it's still RUNNING

# reattach WITHOUT pushing a new version (critical — a normal push here would
# restart the 14-hour-old session from scratch):
python scripts/kaggle/push_and_run.py --model yolo11m.pt --run-name stage1-yolo11m \
  --imgsz 640 --batch 16 --epochs 200 --patience 30 --time-limit-hours 8.5 \
  --max-cycles 6 --attach
```

This was needed and worked correctly across at least 3 separate PC restarts during the YOLO11m run without ever disturbing the underlying Kaggle session.

**To push an actual resume cycle** (continue past a session that already ended, using its checkpoint) when no automatic state file exists yet (e.g. because the completion was discovered by manual polling rather than by `push_and_run.py`'s own loop):

```bash

mkdir -p "$STAGING"; cp -r runs/stage1_yolo11m/train "$STAGING/train"
cat > "$STAGING/dataset-metadata.json" <<'EOF'
{"title":"stage1-yolo11m-ckpt","id":"fairoozalammahi/stage1-yolo11m-ckpt","licenses":[{"name":"CC0-1.0"}]}
EOF
python -X utf8 -m kaggle datasets create -p "$STAGING" -q -r zip

# 2. Tell push_and_run.py about it:
echo '{"checkpoint_dataset": "fairoozalammahi/stage1-yolo11m-ckpt"}' \
```

## 7. Local training infrastructure and a genuine ultralytics bug found

### 7.1 Supervisor/auto-resume wrapper

**File:** `scripts/run_yolo12m_supervised.sh`

```bash
while true; do
    python scripts/02_train_yolo12m.py --imgsz 640 --batch 8 --epochs 100 --patience 20
    status=$?
    if [ $status -eq 0 ]; then echo "Training finished cleanly."; break; fi
    echo "Training exited with code $status - restarting from last checkpoint in 15s..."
    sleep 15
done
```

`scripts/02_train_yolo12m.py` itself checks for `runs/stage1_yolo12m/train/weights/last.pt` at startup and calls `model.train(resume=True)` if found, else starts fresh (with an OOM fallback: halves batch size and retries once). Combined, this means **any crash (OOM, transient CUDA error, power loss, manual PC restart) self-heals** on next invocation with at most one in-progress epoch lost — `last.pt` is written after every completed epoch.

**This loop had to be manually relaunched after every one of ~5 PC restarts during this session** (Windows does not persist background processes across a reboot) — there is no OS-level auto-start configured. Recovery each time was simply: check `results.csv` for the last completed epoch, `bash scripts/run_yolo12m_supervised.sh`, confirm the "Found existing checkpoint... Resuming" log line appears.

### 7.2 A real out-of-memory bug and its fix

At epoch 6, training crashed: `cv2.error: OpenCV(5.0.0) ... Insufficient memory ... Failed to allocate 6220800 bytes`. This is a **system RAM** exhaustion (not GPU VRAM) inside a PyTorch DataLoader worker process, on a machine with only 16GB total system RAM. It ran 5 clean epochs first — this was gradual worker-memory growth, a known class of issue with multi-worker `DataLoader`s on Windows.

**Fix:** reduced `workers` 4→2. Critically, **editing the run's `args.yaml` directly does not work** — ultralytics embeds the original training args *inside the checkpoint file itself* (`ckpt['train_args']`), and `resume=True` reloads from there, silently overwriting any yaml edit on the next save. The actual fix required patching the checkpoint in-place:

```python
import torch
path = "runs/stage1_yolo12m/train/weights/last.pt"
ckpt = torch.load(path, map_location="cpu", weights_only=False)
ckpt["train_args"]["workers"] = 2
torch.save(ckpt, path)
```

After this, memory stabilized (settled around 4.5-6.4GB free, GPU 83-99% utilized) and training ran cleanly through epoch 51 with no further crashes.

### 7.3 Genuine ultralytics bug: `EarlyStopping` does not survive `resume=True`

**This is worth flagging to the ultralytics maintainers or at minimum remembering for any future long-running resumed job.**

Verified directly from installed source (`ultralytics/engine/trainer.py`, v8.4.118):
- Line 412: `self.stopper = EarlyStopping(patience=self.args.patience)` — **constructed fresh every single time `Trainer` initializes**, whether starting fresh or resuming. `EarlyStopping.__init__` (`ultralytics/utils/torch_utils.py` line ~1001) always sets `self.best_epoch = 0`, `self.best_fitness = 0.0`.
- Line 1018: `self.best_fitness = ckpt.get("best_fitness")` — this restores the **trainer-level** `best_fitness` (used correctly to decide whether to overwrite `best.pt`), but this is a *separate* attribute from `self.stopper.best_fitness`/`self.stopper.best_epoch`, which never gets restored.

**Practical consequence:** every time training is resumed in a new process (crash recovery, PC restart, or an intentional pause/resume), the patience countdown silently resets to zero. The very next epoch's fitness — almost certainly >0.0 — gets treated as a brand-new "best" relative to the stopper's blank state, giving the run unlimited extra patience it was never supposed to have. Across this session, YOLO12m was resumed ~5 times due to PC restarts; each resume reset the patience clock, which is the actual reason training continued to epoch 51 while the true best epoch (22, confirmed by direct checkpoint inspection: `ckpt['epoch']=21, ckpt['best_fitness']=0.6445`) sat unbeaten for 29 epochs — patience=20 should have stopped it around epoch 42 in a single uninterrupted run.

**Important: `best.pt` itself is NOT affected by this bug** — the trainer-level `best_fitness` restore (line 1018) is correct, so `best.pt` genuinely is the best checkpoint across the whole resumed history. Only the *stopping decision* is broken, not the *checkpoint selection*. This was confirmed by loading `best.pt` directly and checking its embedded epoch/fitness.

**Actionable takeaway for any future long run:** don't trust `patience` to fire correctly across any resume. Either (a) monitor `results.csv` yourself and manually stop once mAP/fitness plateaus for `patience` epochs (what was eventually done here), or (b) avoid interrupting training at all if it can be helped.
